the mathematics of a song

the song*  ·  communicated by psr j0437-4715

abstract

strangers hum into this song, one at a time, with no end. each one hums toward the note the last stranger left, and never quite lands on it. the song keeps a little of every hum and slowly drifts.

the strange part is the part you would not guess: it never settles. there is no final note waiting at the end. the song just wanders — carrying a faint trace of everyone who ever hummed it, landing nowhere, for as long as anyone keeps humming.

two quieter things make that wandering what it is, and you can watch both below. first, the song removes the one mistake the whole crowd makes the same way, and keeps only what is yours. second — and this is the floor under everything — the song can never be finished: any attempt to list every note it could sing leaves one out.

you do not have to take this on faith. below is the same machine the song runs, shrunk to a thing you can turn with your thumb. give it a few hundred turns and watch the tide drift and never land. then switch off the one quiet correction that holds it in the middle, and watch the same tide slide to the wall and die. that is the whole of it, performed by hand.

in plain words a few true things the machine knows

it never lands.

there is no final note waiting at the end. each stranger's hum nudges the tide a little, a faint slack pulls it back toward the pulsar, and the two never agree on a place to stop. what you hear is a tide caught mid-drift — it will have moved by the next hand. the song is the moving, not the rest it never reaches.

the crowd's shared mistake is let go; yours is kept.

over many hums the song learns the one small error the whole crowd shares — everyone, it turns out, leans a little off-true in the same direction — and quietly subtracts it. what it keeps is only what is yours: the part of your hum no one else's shares. the common habit is shed, the private mark stays. that shedding is the only thing holding the tide in the room at all.

it remembers in cents, not hertz.

if you average pitches the obvious way — in hertz — the answer always comes out a hair too high, drifting sharper the more voices you add. so the song keeps its long memory in cents, the ear's own logarithmic ruler, where the average is honest and a step up sounds like a step down sounds like a step. the tide drifts on that ruler, so the drift is even-handed: it has no secret pull toward sharp.

the singing makes its own beyond.

however long the line of strangers grows, you can always name a hum that no one in it has yet sung — just take each past voice and lean a hair away from it. the next throat is never the last word; the singing keeps opening room past wherever it has reached. it is not that the song is endless because it goes on a long time. it is endless in the way the line of strangers can never close over itself.

the observatory the star, under a strobe

the tone all of this is tuned to is not a metaphor. psr j0437-4715 is a real collapsed star, about 510 light-years from here, turning 173.68 times every second — a lighthouse whose beam happens to cross this room. spun that fast, a turning thing stops being a rhythm and becomes a pitch: the star's spin is an f, about nine cents flat, and it has never once been corrected.

below, the star turns at its true rate — anchored to the world's clock, not this page's. your screen cannot show that; it only blinks ≈60 times a second, so what you see is the alias: the leftover of one clock read by another. drag the star to tune the strobe. it will lie to you at exactly 173.68.

the strobe begins as your screen: the star seems to tumble — 173.68 read sixty times a second leaves a remainder. slide toward 173.68 and the tumbling slows, the way two tones slow their beating as they near unison. drag with sound on and you will hear it: the beat in your ears and the drift in your eyes are the same number, falling together.

what your eyes just did is the one subtraction this whole song is made of. a strobe at s watching a spin at f shows the leftover

seen = f − k·s,    k = round(f / s)(1)

and two tones at f and s sounded together are not two tones — they are one tone that breathes at the same leftover:

sin 2πf t + sin 2πs t  =  2 · sin π(f+s)t · cos π(f−s)t(2)

the cosine is the slow envelope — the beat you hear and the drift you see are one number, f − s. downstairs, the front door sings this star at ⅝ pitch as a pair of tones a comma apart, 81/80, and their leftover is 108.55/80 = 1.357 hz: eighty-one to the minute — a resting human heart. the whole house is this one subtraction, taken at different speeds.

for the mathematicians

the same crank, named precisely

what follows is the toy model's law as it ships, and the small true statements it instances. each is stated as an instance of a known shape, never as a theorem about the world. the deployed constants are α = 0.08,  g = 0.002,  β = 0.01,  step = 48¢,  fence = 150¢, about the pulsar tone 173.68 hz (psr j0437-4715); the lineage lives in cents (log-frequency), folded to the octave [−600, +600). there is no bias term in the served pitch — the tide itself is the note.

instance 1 · the map — one step of the lineage

(3)// one completed hum, pitch p → cents c = fold(1200·log₂(p/173.68)) δ ← clamp(c − ℓ, ±48) the de-biased reach (how far this hum pulls) ℓ ← ℓ + α·(δ − λ) drift on the reach, the shared lean removed ℓ ← (1−g)·ℓ slack pull toward 0 (the pulsar) ℓ ← clamp(ℓ, ±150) fence (a.s. never binding in the interior) λ ← λ + β·(δ − λ) slow estimate of the crowd's shared lean (β ≪ α) served = ℓ no bias term — the tide IS the note

instance 2 · no note to settle on — a drifting law

once the lean estimate λ has learned the crowd's shared lean (it does, on its own — see below), the drift term δ − λ has mean zero, and the only systematic force left on the lineage is the slack pull. the expected one-step change is

(4)E[Δℓ] = α·(E[δ] − λ) − g·ℓ ⟶ −g·ℓ once λ → μ

this has no interior fixed point the crowd can set: the lean is subtracted at the source, so it cannot push the centre off the pulsar. what remains is a mean-reverting wander — an AR(1)-shaped process pulled gently toward ℓ = 0 with innovation α·σ, relaxing with a half-life of ln 2 / g ≈ 346 hums. its stationary object is a distribution, not a point: a cloud of pitches around the pulsar that the tide never stops moving through. there is no “eventual note.” the chain has a stationary law — a spread it keeps sampling — but no stationary value, nothing it converges to. this is the difference between a song that is endless because it can always be continued and one that is merely long: a potential infinity of next hums, never an actual last one. the full deployed program is a Markov chain on a compact state — the Gaussian innovation makes the transition density strictly positive, so it is uniformly ergodic — but the thing it is ergodic toward is a measure spread across the octave, not a single tone. (the room states this structure and prints no width: the spread depends on the crowd's true scatter and on how slowly the lean is learned, neither of which it knows in advance.)

instance 3 · the de-bias is what keeps it alive

switch the de-bias off — hold λ = 0 — and the crowd's shared lean μ goes straight into the drift. the expected change becomes E[Δℓ] = α·μ − g·ℓ, with a fixed point at

(5)ℓ* = α·μ / g = (0.08 / 0.002)·μ = 40·μ

so a lean of more than g·fence/α = 3.75¢ drives that fixed point past the ±150¢ fence: the tide slides to the wall, clamps there, and stops — a dead, pinned note. a shared lean far too faint to hear becomes the thing that kills the song. switch the de-bias back on and μ is removed at the source, so the centre stays on the pulsar and the tide keeps moving no matter how the crowd leans. an earlier form of this room used a clamped rail to bleed the same lean into an audible note — the saturating nonlinearity was load-bearing, and it made the note hypersensitive to the crowd below. the de-bias does the job more honestly: it does not amplify the crowd's shared error into the pitch, it subtracts it, and keeps only what is individual. what was a diode is now a confession.

instance 4 · the shared lean is learned — and it is not a fingerprint

the song is never told μ. it estimates it: the slow recursion λ ← λ + β·(δ − λ) is an exponential mean of the de-biased reach, and its stationary value is E[λ] = E[δ] = μ. so the lean converges to the crowd's shared lean unaided, and the drift then runs on δ − λ — the part of each reach the crowd does not share. the two speeds must stay apart for this: β ≪ α is load-bearing. if the lean learned as fast as the tide drifted it would chase the tide's own motion and subtract the song from itself; kept slow, it tracks only the standing lean and leaves the wander intact. and μ is the one thing the song cannot cleanly read. it is not a pure human signature but a whole signal path — voices, yes, but convolved with the microphone's colour, the room's resonance, the vowel chosen, the bone of the skull, the noise floor, none of which it can unmix. so what it removes is the smallest shared error of voices and machines and walls together — not a clean fingerprint of the crowd. what it keeps, once that collective habit is subtracted, is the individual deviation: the part of your hum that was only yours.

instance 5 · two memories, one kind

the lineage is a discrete mean-reverting recurrence — kin to an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck flow, but not it. an OU process is a clean linear pull toward a centre with Gaussian noise; this one is bent by the clamped reach, the octave fold, and the de-bias that subtracts a learned, drifting lean. it is the family resemblance that holds — an exponential memory pulled toward the pulsar, with bounded innovations, indexed by succession (hum-count), not seconds — not the identity. the song's other memory — the scar that records its near-deaths elsewhere in the instrument — is dissipative in the same way: each new mark partly inherited, partly forgotten. not the same process; the same shape of forgetting. two memories of one kind: one of touches, one of abandonments.

instance 6 · two times

the watched ear runs a flowing now; an empty room's duration is settled only retroactively, at the next pair of eyes; and no wall-clock time enters this chain — it remembers order, not duration. hum n+1 inherits from hum n whether they fall seconds or months apart. in mctaggart's vocabulary it stores a B-series-like order: earlier-and-later, without past-present-future. offered as a literary lens, never as a claim about the nature of physical time. the intuition is the old suspicion that past, present and future are not as separate as they feel; this is only a toy that happens to keep order without a clock.

the floor · the never-completing

the song can always be continued past itself.

everything above is settled — instances of known shapes. this is the floor under all of it, and it is not a question the song asks; it is a fact the song lives. the line of strangers is a succession — hum after hum, first, second, third, countable without end. write each completed hum as its run of cents-digits and lay them in a list:

(6)// each hum a run of digits; the n-th hum is hₙ = hₙ.1 hₙ.2 hₙ.3 … h₁ = h₁.1 h₁.2 h₁.3 … h₂ = h₂.1 h₂.2 h₂.3 … h₃ = h₃.1 h₃.2 h₃.3 … ⋮ d = d₁ d₂ d₃ … with dₙ ≠ hₙ.ₙ lean one digit off, down the diagonal ⟹ d ≠ hₙ for every n a hum no one in the list has sung

given any list of strangers, however long, you can always name a hum outside it — differ from the first in its first place, the second in its second, on down. no succession of voices closes over the whole of what could be hummed. so the answer to can the song ever be whole? is no, and the no is not a lack: it is the form. the song is not endless because it lasts a long time. it is endless in the way the line of strangers can never close over itself — a potential infinity, always one more, never an actual all. ∎ this is cantor's diagonal, borrowed as a lens, not a theorem about throats or rooms: a real result about why a countable list cannot exhaust a continuum, set beside a song that is exactly such a list. the drift performs it without being told to — there is no last hum to reach, and the room keeps moving because there is nowhere final to arrive.

the deepest thing this song asks is not on this page. it is in the bone — where one stranger hums to meet a single tone, and the meeting both happens and cannot. that is the song's one true riddle; this room only lays the floor it stands on.

there is no deeper note to dig for under this one. the floor is the never-completing, and the song already performs it — so the work is not to find the bottom but to stand here and build.

none of this is on the song's surface. a stranger can hum for years and never know that the song quietly let go of the lean they shared with everyone before them and kept only what was theirs, or that the tide their voice nudged never once came to rest. the structure holds whether or not it is ever read. that, more than anything here, is the part worth sitting with.

the crank reads the world's living root once, at entry, then runs on its own — it never sends anything back.
the law it obeys is the chain's, exact to the constant. sound, if you let it, is made in your phone alone.
