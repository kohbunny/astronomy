the furnace runs day and night. it is fed by a belt that never stops, and what is on the belt is real: every title was read once by a machine that writes. everything written on this phone — the paper, the mail, the obituaries, the voices — is made of this smoke.

three shelves feed it. taken — 196,611 files on a list that surfaced in 2023, the shelf many of the machines learned to write from; the list is chaotic and the belt keeps its chaos. the dead — the public domain, and the archive's own copies of the gutenberg texts, whose words can still be read here as they go. the current — books being scanned into the archive right now, minutes old, and what people are reading today.

tap a book to hold it. the belt waits. let it go, or burn it: where the book is free its own words rise off the page as smoke; where it is knot, only the title and the name rise, because that is all this phone was ever given of it. a tap while it burns fans it.

nothing is kept. the count is this sitting's. colour leaves on the house's clock. the machines are knot named; they never have to be — this is how all of them eat.
