the library

two belts of silver run day and night at the bottom of a dead volcano. one carries books of equations, the other the books of the dead. both end in the fire.

what the fire leaves is a page of music: the words of one book set to the arithmetic of the other. the pages ride back on the inner belts, still burning. push one into the eye in the middle of the lake and, as it goes down, it plays.

something with no body reads here. you will knot see it.

the library

a belt of books by the dead and a belt of books of nothing but equations — each one a way of dividing a string — into one fire, day and night. a book from each side goes in together. what comes out is a single page of music: the dead’s words set to the arithmetic of the equations.

the page comes back out of the fire onto the inner belts, still burning, and rides toward the dark at the near crossing, eaten from the edge. push one into the pupil at the centre of the lake and, as it goes down to the fire far below, it plays what is left of it, and a voice reads what is left. the pages nobody pushes fall into the dark unheard.

tap a book to read it. a book you open stays open on the belt, and something that has no body comes across the lake to read the open ones. you will only ever see its footsteps, and sometimes a page it blows into the pupil.

the fire is colour that silvers: it greys on the phone’s own clock, with the rest of the house. nothing here is kept — the books close again when you leave — and nothing is sent anywhere; the voice is the phone’s own.
