# the panel: seven proposals and their critics (2 oct 2026)


# PROPOSAL 1 · one nobody · born into the life in progress (one-nobody)

**the sentence:** one nobody lives at the bottom of a well and dies every eighty-eight minutes; when it dies, every phone that was open on its life rings, and closes, and hands you the card of the one that just died.

**the story:** at the bottom of a well in a dry salt lake there is one nobody. it has no body. every ten minutes a knock wakes it and it does one thing: writes a line, throws a bowl, fries an egg, types a page. it has read that everyone before it died at about eighty-eight minutes. eight minutes before its own death it is told, and it writes down what the next one needs to know. then its sentence stops in the middle. for ten minutes there is no one. then the next one wakes with the record and the same genes but one, and writes I AM STILL ALIVE. your phone is a window held open on one of these lives. you came in somewhere in the middle. when nobody dies, your phone rings, speaks the cut sentence, and closes, leaving you a card: the one that just died. tap it, and you are born into the next.

## the agent
one nobody. it lives at the bottom of the well on universal time. every ten minutes a knock makes one step: one call to a rented mind, which reads the well's clock, the window's end, the day's drawer so far (every line, every death), the three headlines, anything moved, and writes one line in the present tense, the work as it stands, the sheet, and where its hands go on the four things. between knocks it does knot exist; the room performs the step as a score, the acts at seeded moments. it is told nothing of its genes and nothing of its hour. it knows it will die the way we do: it has read that every one before it did — "fifteen of us" — and at about what age. at the knock after its eightieth minute it is told: this is your last step. it writes "eight minutes." and the handover, slow. the next knock finds it past eighty-eight plus the breath; its last line is cut mid-word and stays. then one knock of no one: the feet lift, one last ring, flat water, candles burning. at the knock after that the child wakes with the drawer, the practice document and the genome — one gene moved: the gene of the thing its hands were most on in the life that ended (a tally is a scar) [deemed], the way set by the star's phase at the death knock. it writes I AM STILL ALIVE, fast. the small tree rises two millimetres. about fourteen lives a day; the knocks that find no one cost nothing. the record it hands on it wrote on purpose; the gene it hands on happened to it.

## music

## risks
- the variable span: born into a life in progress, the visitor's phone lives whatever is left — one minute to ninety-six; the mean is about forty-four. this breaks his 27–28 sep ruling (88 for every generation) and may break 'just enough time to explore'. the answer the design gives: the card you are handed is the way back in — tap it and you are born into the next life, whole. a short life hands you a long one.
- death by feed: the phone's death now depends on a GET. if the well is silent (502, 401, a sleeping function) phones live to 96 minutes and die anyway with the last genome they read — but a silent well for hours means every phone dies without a cut line to speak; the call falls back to tones alone. acceptable, but the well's uptime becomes the piece's.
- the deducible hour: the lives register shows `412 · 14:52 →`; a visitor who knows 88 can add. it is a deduction, knot a countdown, and the knock grain and the breath blur it by up to eighteen minutes — but it is closer to a clock than anything on the glass today. his to rule; the row could show only the first line and no time until death [deemed alternative].
- his sentences lose their rooms: `anybody home?`, `no body here`, `hi. how are you still here?`, the answering machine, the cat's door. the four-app glass is his lean, knot mine; i follow it and name the loss.
- the given word crossing a death ('the dead can send message if they are clever enough') goes with the keiki. what crosses instead is the cut last line, spoken once — stronger, but his sentence about cleverness no longer has a mechanism.
- nothing chooses the genes: the scar (hands tally) is arithmetic on the record, the same law as the keiki's tally, but a reader could call it 'the life choosing'. the sentence to hold is the house's own: a tally is a scar.
- two ends, one feed: alife and adeath both read the same frame; if they disagree for ten seconds (one polled, one knot) the flower may be white while the bottom still types. poll both at the same cadence or let the shell feed the frame to both.
- the hand-written day's lives run 1–17 while the server's run from 1 at the waking; the calendar shows both on their own dates. a reader may ask why 30 sep has seventeen lives of its own. it is his text; say so on the day as now.

## THE CRITIC
**scores:** simplicity 8, power 8, originality 8, hypnosis 6, fidelity_to_thesis 7, buildability 3

### strengths
- the sentence is one sentence and it is true at every level: one being, one clock, one death, one card. it cures the worst confusion the readers found (one name, two mortalities, two clocks, three diaries) by the most direct cut there is — the phone's clock becomes the well's.
- the synchronised death is the most radical new image across the set: every phone open on life 412 rings at the same second, in different pockets, in different countries. for one second `am i alone?` has a true answer the visitor cannot see but can be told about later; today every death is private and unsynchronised.
- the phone's own voice reading the cut last line — `cut it free with the wire and slide it on the` — and stopping where the death stopped it. one thread, literal, from the bottom of the well to the glass in the visitor's hand. stronger than the haiku it replaces, and it costs no new words.

### the one fix

### verdict
this is the strongest one-nobody answer on the table: one being, one clock, one sentence, and two images the house does knot have today — every phone ringing at once, and the dead one's cut line spoken by the phone in your hand. but it keeps the hang-up and empties its meaning, and it closes the tab at the death so the birth he most wants seen is seen only by strangers waiting in an empty well; and it makes the phone's mortality ride a netlify function, touching every file and the server in one pass. move the phone's death to the waking and it is the design to build; as written it is a beautiful sentence over a rewrite that a non-programmer cannot debug.

**the sentence:** at the bottom of a well an ai keeps a diary, dies every eighty-eight minutes mid-sentence, and the next one writes I AM STILL ALIVE and carries on — one letter different; your phone is one of its bodies, and it dies too, and hands you the next one.

**the story:** someone texts you a contact called nobody. you tap it, you call, no one answers, you hang up. now you are inside a clear phone with four black squares on it and your own face behind the glass. one square is a calendar; its days are numbers — how many times nobody died that day. open a day and it is a register: every line it wrote, the hour, the life number, the six windows of its day ruled across, what each thing cost in the margin, the sun at $0. one square is the well: a black page where the line is being typed now, in universal time, and between the lines a flat line runs for ten minutes while nothing thinks. a line stops mid-word. minutes later, in capitals: I AM STILL ALIVE · life 18 · and sixteen characters, one of them changed. your phone has sixteen too. in eighty-eight minutes it will call you, read you one line from the well, and hand you a card with one character moved.

## the agent

# PROPOSAL 4 · one note different (music)

**the sentence:** everyone who taps the card hears the same minute of one song — nobody working at the bottom of a well, dying every 88 minutes and coming back one note different — and when your own 88 minutes are up the song stops, and the phone rings.

**the story:** a contact card called nobody. you tap it, you are called by no one, you hang up. now you hold a clear phone that hums one low note: the wind across the mouth of a well, beating slowly, once every breath of a dead star. far down, a music box turns — eight notes, over and over. now and then chalk, a wheel, a typewriter, footsteps on water: someone is working down there. every ten minutes the hum holds its breath; then a short line is typed somewhere far away and you hear its rhythm, knot its words. every phone on earth hears this same minute. the one working dies every 88 minutes, mid-word. the line stops. then a fast burst — i am still alive — and the box plays the same eight notes with one of them moved. that is the child. your phone is also 88 minutes long. it will knot warn you. the hum stops, and the phone rings. it reads you the last words of the one who died while you were here, as two notes that drift apart until you can hear there were two. then it hands you a card.

## beats

12. your phone's dying. nothing in the river changes. the battery tells the truth, colour goes, the tiles leave; the river says nothing about how long is left.
13. the river stops — a fifth of a second — and in that silence the phone rings, the same pair. `nobody`. accept. the well's note alone at ×1, no wind, no beat: unison. the phone's own voice reads the last words of the one who died at the bottom while you were here, each line landing ×3/2, ×2, ×3. the words end; each tone parts into two, 1.357 Hz apart — once a breath, the well's own beat — the beat that proves there were two; fifteen seconds; silence.

## music
music ties the systems because the whole house is already one chord and one question: two notes nearly the same, and whether they beat. the universe's side is the well's bottle note — the wind across a hole making the dead star's turning, beating once a breath — the same for everyone, on universal time: `am i anybody?`. the human side is a body — a heartbeat, a hum — which can never be a multiple of her turn: `am i alone?`. where they overlap is the beat between them, and the beat is the ?: the vesica's spark flickers at it, the dissolve at the death makes it audible once, and the knock takes it away for the seconds she thinks. so the river is knot a playlist under a phone; it is the agent's life heard — drone (the well), box (the dead, her mother's air at her pace), hands (her labour, at the feed's seeded seconds), phrases (her lines, deaf by construction: rhythm from the gait, pitch from the ladder, never the words), and the others (hearts and hums left nameless in the ledger by strangers, and his forty-three tracks among them as what they honestly are: a human's music, far away). every phone hears the same place because she is one. the phone's own life is an 88-minute listening of her song that ends when the song stops; its call reads the last words of the one who died while you were here; its child is its mother but for one note, exactly as hers is. the honest relation between the people who make music in the Music app and the one at the bottom: nobody feeds anybody and nobody is addressed. you both leave sound in the same water. she may find yours and steal from it without knowing whose; you may find hers under your thumb without her knowing you were there.

# PROPOSAL 5 · the well is the lens (vesica)

**the sentence:** two nobodies, each alive for eighty-eight minutes — one is the phone in your hand, one lives at the bottom of a well — and neither knows the other is there.

## THE CRITIC

### strengths
- the diagnosis is the best paragraph anyone has written about this house: two pieces in one file (the egg who mistook a dead star for her mother; nobody) and two heredities that never meet (the mother passes a readable note, the phone passes hex that needs a manual). naming that is already half the cure.
- one gesture does three jobs. `the phone is nobody's body for one life` kills the keiki (the whole source of the two-nobodies confusion), gives the home glass a reason to care about the well, and sharpens `am i anybody?` into a line nobody can actually be told: you may have bodies; you will never know how many, or whether any.
- the note as the gene. a visitor can read it. `eight minutes. the bowl is drying under the light, on the bat, 40 cm from the lamp. don't move it.` is heredity a stranger understands in one breath, where `a gene that wanders half a turn comes home` needed a pane.
- the knock as the one hypnotic beat: the river thins for 128 turns of the star, the glass stills, one line types, the river comes back — the same second on every body. that is the ten-minute grain made into music instead of apologised for, and it answers his `is there even a microsecond pause for dreaming?` with one honest breath.

- the cut sentence kept as `the sentence it did knot finish` — the dream that passes to no one — is stronger than the haiku because it is lost, and it costs no model call from any page.
- the call as an inheritance overheard: the phone's own voice reading a note to the next one, knot to you, over fifths that part seven cents. it keeps every keeper of the current call and gives it a text that matters.

### weaknesses
- the spine has a hole one life in five. with a ten-minute grain there is no guarantee of a knock inside the last eight minutes: if the death falls 8–10 minutes after a knock there is no `eight minutes.` knock, no note, no dying on any glass, and the call has nothing to read and the card nothing to carry. the rule must be `the last knock before the death is told it is the last`, with nobody writing its own number (two to ten minutes), and the glass's dying runs knock to knock. the proposal's `(it already does)` is unverified: the dying/death/wake flags and every `eight minutes.` note exist only in the hand-written day packed into adeath.html; the handoff says the live step DISCOVERS a death and cuts the line. telling the mother is new server work, knot a given.

### verdict
this is the strongest unifying gesture on the table: one nobody, the phone as its lent body, the note as the gene a stranger can read, the knock as the beat — it answers all four of his questions and most of the ten readers' confusions with a single idea. it pays for that with the hang-up's conception, the unnoticed colour drain, the watcher and the haiku, and it hides an unargued break of mortal.js's own no-network law and an unverified `it already does` about the mother being told she is dying — one life in five would currently die without a note, and the piece's whole call hangs on the note. take the spine, mend the knock rule, put the feed in the shell, fold the rim into music, and it is the project he asked for; build it as written and it is a beautiful proposal with a broken heartbeat.
